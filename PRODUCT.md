# Product: Sara Kim — Personal Atlas

## Platform and audience

Sara Kim's personal website is a public, writing-led web experience for friends, readers, collaborators, and other curious visitors who want to understand her work, interests, and life across places.

## Purpose

The site is a digital map of a person in progress. It introduces Sara, houses her writing, and gradually turns geography into an index for photographs, poems, notes, and other memories.

## Current product shape

- The homepage introduces Sara and the places that have shaped her.
- A blog provides the existing long-form publishing surface.
- The first atlas release adds an interactive homepage globe with explorable place markers.
- Memory detail pages are a future capability; this release must not imply that every marker already has a finished destination.

## Positioning

This is a personal editorial archive, not a portfolio dashboard or travel-tracking product. Place is treated as memory and narrative rather than as analytics. The experience should reward curiosity while remaining calm enough for reading.

## Brand commitments

- Personal, thoughtful, literary, and quietly playful.
- Generous negative space and restrained interaction.
- A soft green, paper-like visual world named **Soft Atlas**.
- Contemporary editorial craft rather than glossy, futuristic, or startup-oriented styling.
- Typography and motion should support the writing, never compete with it.

## Product principles

1. **Place is an index.** Geographic markers should be ready to grow into memory collections without coupling map presentation to page markup.
2. **Writing stays primary.** The globe is an invitation into the archive, not a replacement for Sara's introduction or essays.
3. **Calm is a feature.** Movement is slow, direct manipulation takes precedence over auto-motion, and reduced-motion preferences are respected.
4. **Progressive disclosure.** A visitor can understand the page immediately, then hover, tap, drag, or use the place index for detail.
5. **Honest availability.** Locations without memory pages remain explorable but do not masquerade as completed links.
